## A Promise Made in the Middle of the Siege

There's a detail in Isaiah 33 that I almost read past, and it changed the whole chapter for me.

The verse everyone quotes is verse 6: "He will be the stability of your times, abundance of salvation, wisdom, and knowledge; the fear of the LORD is Zion's treasure." Beautiful. Frameable. The kind of thing you put on a mug.

But look at where it sits. Verse 1 — the destroyer is still destroying. Verse 8 — the treaty-breakers are still breaking treaties. Verse 9 — the highways lie waste, the land itself is mourning. Assyria, the superpower of the age, is at the gates.

And right there, in the teeth of all that, God says: *I will be the stability of your times.*

Not when the Assyrians leave. Not once you've figured things out. Not after the pressure lifts. *These* times. The unstable ones. The ones you're living through right now.

That's the scandal of this verse, and it's worth sitting with before we go any further: God doesn't wait for your circumstances to improve before offering Himself as the centre. He offers Himself as the stability *in* the chaos — not after it.

## He Doesn't Give Stability. He Is It.

Read the promise again, slowly, and notice what God doesn't say. He doesn't say "I will give you stability," or "I will help you stabilise your life." He says *He will be* the stability.

That's not a grammar quirk. It's the whole point.

The Hebrew word here is *emunah* — the same root as *amen*. Firmness. Faithfulness. Steadiness. The thing you lean your weight on and it holds. And the verse locates all of that not in a plan, a system, or a set of circumstances, but in a Person.

Which means the stability God offers is categorically different from the kind we spend our lives engineering. We build contingency plans. We diversify. We keep backup options warm. None of that is wrong — but none of it is a foundation, either. It's scaffolding, and scaffolding comes down.

What makes your times stable is not the absence of threats. It's the presence of a faithful God. Judah learned this with an empire camped outside the walls. Most of us learn it the same way — not in the calm, but in the siege.

## What Comes in the Package

The verse doesn't stop at stability. It unfolds a sequence — stability, abundance of salvation, wisdom, knowledge — and the order isn't random. It's the anatomy of divine security, each piece flowing from the one before it.

**Abundance of salvation.** Not just salvation — *abundance* of it. The Hebrew word, *yeshu'ah*, is the root of the name Yeshua. Jesus. It means deliverance, rescue, help — and the verse insists there's more of it than you need.

I don't know about you, but I catch myself praying like God's help is rationed. Like I've used up my quota of rescues and should save my remaining requests for emergencies. This verse dismantles that. The well doesn't run dry. You can come back tomorrow, next month, next year, and He is still abundant in deliverance. Where have you stopped asking because you quietly decided He's done enough?

**Wisdom and knowledge.** These two travel together in Isaiah. Wisdom is the skill of living rightly — knowing *how* to move. Knowledge is seeing reality as it actually is — not through the fog of fear or wishful thinking.

And here's what I find striking: stability doesn't produce passivity. It produces clarity. When you're anchored, you don't panic — you perceive. You make better decisions, not because you're smarter, but because you're no longer deciding from fear. The person whose mind is under siege can't see straight. The person whose centre holds can.

## The Treasure Nobody Can Steal

The verse ends with a line we tend to skim: "the fear of the LORD is Zion's treasure."

It's the capstone of the whole promise — and I'd argue it's the prerequisite, not the afterthought. Stability, salvation, wisdom, knowledge: all of it flows from this one thing. You don't get divine stability while treating God casually.

But let's be careful with the word "fear." This isn't terror, and it isn't anxiety. It's also more than the polite "reverence" we've domesticated it into. The fear of the LORD carries an element of trembling awe — the visceral recognition that you are dealing with Someone who holds your next breath, who will not be managed, predicted, or controlled. He is not your life coach. He is the King of the universe who happens to love you.

C.S. Lewis caught it in that famous line about Aslan — "'Course he isn't safe. But he's good." That's the fear of the LORD in a sentence. Not safe. Good. And that combination is exactly what makes Him trustworthy when His ways run past your comprehension.

Here's why Isaiah calls it a *treasure*, and this undid me a little: almost everything else we treasure can be taken. Health, money, reputation, people — all of it sits exposed to invaders and exile and time. The fear of the LORD is the one possession that cannot be taken from you. It can only be surrendered.

Which raises the question worth journaling on: what do you currently protect, prioritise, and panic over losing? Because *that* — not what you claim on Sunday — is your functional treasure.

## The Counterfeit: Why Control Isn't Stability

Every real promise has a counterfeit, and this one's counterfeit is everywhere: control.

The person who needs every variable locked down, every outcome predicted, every risk eliminated looks stable from the outside. They're not. They're rigid — and rigidity shatters under pressure. God's stability is flexible strength: you bend in the storm and you don't break, because your root system isn't in the storm's reach.

The culture we live in sells the counterfeit relentlessly. Engineer your security through wealth, productivity, strategic relationships, personal branding — the gospel of self-sufficiency. And Isaiah 33:6 quietly demolishes it. The moment you believe you can generate your own security is the moment you stop drawing on His.

This isn't a call to passivity. You still work, plan, save, build. But the load-bearing beam of your life can't be those things — and you find out what your load-bearing beam actually is the day something you were counting on fails. If you built on self-sufficiency, you collapse with it. If you built on God, you grieve the loss and remain standing.

The disciples learned this in a boat. Storm raging, water coming in, and Jesus asleep on a cushion. They wake Him in a panic, He stills the sea with a word — and then He asks them the question that matters: why were you so afraid?

The storm was never the real issue. The issue was whether the Person in the boat was enough to count as stability. That's Isaiah 33:6 told as a story — and most of us are still in the boat, learning the same thing.

## What This Looks Like on a Tuesday

Meditation that doesn't change your Tuesday morning is just sentiment. So let's land it.

**Money is tight, or an expense just blindsided you.** The counterfeit response: panic, obsessive contingency planning, maybe a corner cut to secure the outcome. The Isaiah 33:6 response: you still do the math and make the plan — but the emotional centre of your life isn't in the account balance. You sleep at night not because the numbers add up but because He is your stability.

**Someone betrayed you, or walked away.** The counterfeit: control the narrative, defend the reputation, cut them off to feel in charge again. The promise: you grieve honestly, you set what boundaries are needed — but your identity was never in their perception of you, so you can afford to forgive. You don't need to win the story.

**You're in the vocational fog.** The door didn't open. You don't know the next step. The counterfeit: frantic networking fuelled by anxiety, maybe values bent to force an outcome. The promise: you do the work — apply, reach out, steward what's in front of you — but from peace, not panic. Because the stability of your times was never the job. It's the God who holds your times.

Same actions, you'll notice. Completely different centre of gravity.

**A question to sit with this week:**

If God audited your life right now — your calendar, your bank account, your thought patterns, the things that keep you up at night — what would He identify as the thing actually functioning as "the stability of your times"?

And if the honest answer isn't Him — what's one thing you'd need to hand over this week to change that?

He will be the stability of your times. Not might be. Not could be. *Will be.* Rest there.
