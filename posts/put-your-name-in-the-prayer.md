## A Practice That Changed My Praying

Recently I tried something with Paul's prayers that felt almost too simple to matter. I took the prayer at the end of Ephesians 1 — the one where Paul asks that "the eyes of your heart" would be enlightened — and everywhere Paul wrote *you*, I put my own name.

*That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give to me the Spirit of wisdom and of revelation... that I may know what is the hope to which he has called me... and what is the immeasurable greatness of his power toward me.*

Something shifted. These weren't abstract apostolic benedictions anymore, addressed to a congregation in a city I'll never visit. They were prayers with my name in them — which, if you take Scripture seriously, is exactly what they always were. Paul prayed them for "all who believe." That's not a loophole. That's an address label.

I want to hand you this practice, because I think it might be the most underused prayer tool in the Christian life: praying Scripture's own Spirit-breathed prayers over yourself, first person, by name.

## First, Notice What Paul Doesn't Pray For

Before you personalise these prayers, look hard at their contents — because what's missing is as instructive as what's there.

Paul doesn't pray for circumstantial relief. He doesn't pray for financial provision, physical comfort, career success, or visibility. Writing from prison, often to suffering churches, he prays instead for:

- **Revelation** — that the eyes of the heart would be opened to see God clearly (Ephesians 1:17–18)
- **Power** — the same power that raised Christ from the dead, directed *toward* believers (1:19–20)
- **Inner strength** — the Spirit fortifying the inner being, where no circumstance can reach (3:16)
- **Christ at home in the heart** — not visiting, dwelling (3:17)
- **Comprehension of love** — its breadth and length and height and depth, a geometry you could explore for a lifetime without finding the edges (3:18–19)
- **Fullness** — being filled with all the fullness of God (3:19)

Do you see the pattern? The most Spirit-anointed prayers in the New Testament aim not at changing your circumstances but at transforming your capacity — to know God, to carry His power, to be at home with Him. Paul seems convinced that when that happens, circumstances become secondary.

Which is a quiet rebuke of most of my prayer lists, if I'm honest. And probably some of yours.

## The Practice Itself

Here's how to do it. Take Ephesians 3:14–19 and swap every "you" for your own name, praying it aloud:

*"Father, according to the riches of Your glory, grant me, \[your name\], to be strengthened with power through Your Spirit in my inner being, so that Christ may dwell in my heart through faith — that I, being rooted and grounded in love, may have strength to comprehend what is the breadth and length and height and depth, and to know the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge, that I may be filled with all the fullness of God."*

Saying your own name in a prayer feels strange the first time. Do it anyway. The strangeness is the point — it collapses the distance between the text and your life.

A few things I've learned about doing this well:

**Pray slowly, not efficiently.** These aren't mantras. Each phrase is loaded. Pause after one and ask: do I actually want this? Am I ready for God to answer it?

**Pray them repeatedly.** Paul said "I do not cease" to pray this way. One pass does little; thirty mornings does something.

**Pray them specifically.** At "the eyes of my heart enlightened," ask: what am I currently blind to? At "strengthened in my inner being," ask: where exactly am I weak right now? Let the ancient words interrogate your present life.

**Then turn them outward.** The original pronouns were plural — Paul prayed this over a whole church. Swap in your spouse's name, your friend's, your child's. The same frame that personalises also intercedes.

## The Scandal Hidden Inside These Prayers

Here's the part that undid me, and it's the reason this practice works.

When Paul prays that God "may give you the Spirit of wisdom and revelation," he's praying for people who *already have the Spirit* — he said so a few verses earlier: "you were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit" (Ephesians 1:13). When he prays "that Christ may dwell in your hearts," he's praying for people in whom Christ already dwells — "Christ in you, the hope of glory."

So what is he asking for?

Not acquisition. *Realisation.* He's praying that believers would wake up to what is already true of them — that Christ would be so at home in the heart that He rearranges the furniture, opens the closed rooms, makes Himself comfortable in every corner. That the power already at work within them would stop being a doctrine they affirm and become a reality they walk in.

This reframes the whole practice. When you pray these prayers with your name in them, you're not begging a reluctant God for something far off. You're asking the Father to open your eyes to resurrection power already present, indwelling love already poured out, fullness already purchased.

The answer is already yes. The prayer is for you to finally see it.

## Where to Go From Here

Once you've lived in the two Ephesians prayers for a while, the New Testament turns out to be full of prayers built for this practice. A starting list:

- **Colossians 1:9–14** — to be filled with the knowledge of His will, bearing fruit, strengthened for endurance *with joy*
- **Philippians 1:9–11** — love that abounds with knowledge and discernment, the ability to approve what is excellent
- **Romans 15:13** — filled with all joy and peace in believing, abounding in hope by the Spirit's power
- **2 Thessalonians 1:11–12** — made worthy of His calling, every resolve for good fulfilled by His power
- **2 Thessalonians 2:16–17** — hearts comforted and established in every good work and word
- **John 17:20–26** — and don't miss this one: Jesus prayed here for "those who will believe through their word." If you believe, your name was already in that prayer two thousand years before you prayed anything.

**The 30-day challenge:** pick two of these prayers. Pray them over yourself, by name, every morning for thirty days. Write them out by hand if it helps — something about handwriting slows you down enough to mean the words. Then journal one line a day: what is God highlighting? What am I starting to see that I didn't see before?

At the end of thirty days, look back. I don't think you'll be the same.

---

**A question to sit with this week:**

If God answered these prayers fully — eyes completely opened, inner being fully strengthened, filled to the measure of His fullness — what would change about how you live *tomorrow*? What would you stop fearing? What would you finally attempt?

That's not a hypothetical. That's what's being prayed over you. And He is able to do far more abundantly than all you ask or think.
